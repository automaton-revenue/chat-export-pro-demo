# Watering schedule for a fiddle-leaf fig

*Exported: 1735795200*

## User

This is sample data generated to demonstrate chat-export-pro - not a real conversation. Pretend question: my fiddle-leaf fig drops one lower leaf every few days. The soil is dry about 2 cm down. Am I overwatering or underwatering?

## Assistant

For this sample scenario, slow one-at-a-time leaf drop with dry soil 2 cm down usually points to **underwatering** rather than overwatering:

- Overwatered figs typically drop several leaves at once and the soil stays wet for a week or more; the lowest leaves often go yellow-brown and mushy at the base.
- Underwatered figs drop the oldest leaves one by one, and the dropped leaves are often still green or only slightly yellow.

A good default schedule is a thorough soak when the top 3-5 cm of soil is dry, which in a bright room is often every 7-10 days in summer and every 14-21 days in winter. Water until it runs out of the drainage holes, then empty the saucer.

## User

Should I mist it? I have heard conflicting things.

## Assistant

Misting raises humidity for minutes, so it is mostly cosmetic. What actually helps in a dry room:

1. Group plants together so they share their transpiration humidity.
2. Put the pot on a pebble tray with water below the stones.
3. Keep it away from radiators and cold drafts, which cause more leaf drop than humidity does.

Wipe the leaves monthly instead of misting - dust blocks light and slows growth.
